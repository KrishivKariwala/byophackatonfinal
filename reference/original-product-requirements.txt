Food Rescue Marketplace — PRD
23 Nov 2026 · @ai team 10
1. Summary and problem
RescueBite (working name) is a hyperlocal, two-sided marketplace. Restaurants and bakeries list same-day surplus food at 50–70% off. Nearby customers reserve it and pick it up in a set window. Food left unclaimed late in the day goes to verified charities for free. MVP target: 1 city (Bengaluru), 300 vendors, 15,000 rescued meals a month within 6 months of launch.
The problem. India discards an estimated 78.1 million tonnes of food a year, about 55 kg per person in households alone (UNEP Food Waste Index 2024 via Statista). Food service is the second-largest source. A bakery or restaurant typically has 5–15% of daily prep left at closing. Today that food is binned, given informally to staff, or given away with no tracking.

Core design decisions. (1) Two listing types: Surprise Bag (fast, contents vary) and Itemised (named dishes, exact counts). (2) Pay at reservation, not at pickup, to cut no-shows. (3) A charity cascade: unreserved items auto-offer to charities 45 minutes before the pickup window closes. (4) Pickup only in MVP; no delivery, which keeps cost and food-safety risk low.
2. Goals, non-goals and success metrics
The north-star metric is meals rescued per week (paid pickups + charity pickups that completed handover).
Goals (MVP, first 6 months)
1.  Vendors recover revenue on food that would otherwise be binned.
2.  Customers get good food at 50–70% off within 3 km of them.
3.  Charities get reliable, scheduled access to food that would otherwise go unsold.
4.  Every rescued meal is counted and reported as impact (kg food, kg CO2e).
Non-goals (MVP)
• Delivery or courier logistics
• Grocery and supermarket chains (phase 2)
• Cooked food older than the same service day
• Customer-to-customer resale or sharing

5. User flows
The loop has three lanes that meet at one moment: the vendor scanning the pickup QR. That scan closes the order for paid and charity pickups alike, and triggers payout and impact counting.
Customer flow, step by step
1.  Open app → feed shows listings within 3 km, sorted by pickup time.
2.  Filter (e.g. Veg, Bakery, pickup after 8 pm) → open a listing.
3.  Pick quantity → stock is held for 5 min → pay by UPI.
4.  Get pickup pass (QR + 4-digit code) and a calendar-style countdown.
5.  Reminder when the window opens and 15 min before it ends.
6.  Tap "I'm on my way" → walk in → show QR → vendor scans.
7.  Order shows "Collected" → rate the food → impact profile updates.
Vendor flow, step by step
1.  Around 1–2 hours before close, tap "Quick list" on the tablet.
2.  Choose a template (e.g. "Evening Bread Box") → set quantity → confirm window → Publish.
3.  Reservations arrive with a sound alert and appear in the Reserved column.
4.  Pack each order, label it with the customer's first name and code → tap Packed.
5.  Customer arrives → scan QR or type code → order moves to Collected.
6.  At T−45 min, unsold units offer automatically to NGOs; NGO collects with the same scan.
7.  End of day: summary of units sold, donated, revenue and payout date.
Charity flow, step by step
1.  NGO gets a push: "6 meals available at Ravi's Bakery, collect by 9:30 pm".
2.  Claims all or part → receives a collection pass.
3.  Volunteer collects → vendor scans → donation record issued to both sides.
Edge cases the flows must handle
• Customer late or no-show → grace of 15 min, then No-show; vendor keeps payment; unit can go to charity.
• Vendor runs out after reservations → vendor picks which orders to cancel; auto-refund + apology credit.
• Payment succeeds but app crashes → idempotent callback still creates the reservation; pass appears on next open.
• Vendor tablet offline → scans queue locally and sync within 10 min.
6. Functional requirements
Each requirement has an ID for traceability into tickets and QA.
6.1 Listings
• FR-L1 A listing has: type (Itemised / Surprise Bag), title, photo (optional for bags), original price, rescue price, quantity, veg/non-veg flag, allergens, prepared-at time, pickup window (start, end), pickup instructions.
• FR-L2 Rescue price must be ≤ 50% of original price and ≥ ₹49. The system blocks publishing outside these limits.
• FR-L3 Pickup window must start ≥ 15 min after publish, last 30–180 min, and end before the store's closing time + 30 min.
• FR-L4 Cooked meals must have a prepared-at time within the same service day. Listings expire automatically at window end.
• FR-L5 Vendors can save up to 20 templates. A template prefills everything except quantity and window.
6.2 Live availability
• FR-A1 Available quantity = listed − reserved − held. It is the single source of truth, stored server-side.
• FR-A2 Changes to quantity reach all open clients within 2 s (p95) through WebSocket, with polling fallback every 15 s.
• FR-A3 Reservation uses an atomic decrement; it can never take available quantity below 0.
• FR-A4 If a vendor reduces stock below reserved quantity, the system asks which reservations to cancel and refunds them automatically.
6.3 Reservations and payment
• FR-R1 Checkout holds stock for 5 min. Unpaid holds are released automatically.
• FR-R2 Payment is captured at reservation through UPI, cards or wallets via a payment gateway (e.g. Razorpay).
• FR-R3 Maximum 4 units per listing per customer, to deter resale.
• FR-R4 Customer can cancel for a full refund until 60 min before window start. Later cancellation gets a 50% platform credit.
• FR-R5 Vendor cancellation always refunds 100% and counts against the vendor's reliability score.
• FR-R6 No-show (not collected by window end + 15 min grace): no refund, vendor is paid, food may go to charity.
6.4 Pickup tracking and handover
• FR-P1 Each reservation gets a pickup pass with a QR code and a 4-digit code.
• FR-P2 Order states: Reserved → Packed → Ready → Collected, or Cancelled / No-show. Every change notifies the customer.
• FR-P3 The customer can tap "I'm on my way"; the vendor sees an ETA badge (approximate, from location if shared).
• FR-P4 Handover is confirmed only by the vendor scanning the QR or entering the code. This closes the order and starts settlement.
• FR-P5 If the vendor is closed or the food is missing, the customer can raise "Couldn't collect" from the pass for a full refund after admin review.
6.5 Charity cascade
• FR-C1 At T−45 min before window end (vendor-configurable 15–120 min), unreserved units are offered free to verified NGOs within 5 km.
• FR-C2 Preferred NGOs get first refusal for 10 min, then the offer opens to all eligible NGOs.
• FR-C3 Once claimed, units are removed from the customer feed.
• FR-C4 Charity handover uses the same QR flow; the vendor receives a donation record per collection.
6.6 Vendor dashboard
• FR-V1 Home shows today: live listings, units left, orders by state, next pickups in the coming 30 min, revenue today.
• FR-V2 Orders board updates in real time and works offline for 10 min, syncing scans when back online.
• FR-V3 Earnings: gross sales, commission, refunds, net payout, payout date; CSV export.
• FR-V4 Impact: meals rescued, kg food (default 0.4 kg per meal unless vendor overrides), kg CO2e (2.5 kg per kg food, configurable).

8. Architecture and data model
A modular backend behind one API gateway, with a WebSocket hub pushing stock changes. The inventory service owns every stock count, which is what prevents oversell.
Suggested stack: React Native (customer + vendor apps), Next.js (vendor web dashboard, admin), Node.js/TypeScript or Go services, PostgreSQL + PostGIS, Redis, a managed queue (SQS / Pub/Sub), Razorpay for payments and payouts (Route), Firebase Cloud Messaging + SMS fallback, Google Maps Platform.

Food safety (must-have before launch)
• Every vendor must hold a valid FSSAI licence or registration, checked at onboarding and re-checked on expiry.
• Listings show prepared-at time, allergens and storage note ("keep refrigerated", "eat within 4 hours").
• Cooked food can only be listed for the same service day; high-risk items (dairy desserts, seafood, rice dishes) have a 4-hour maximum listing age.
• NGO donations follow FSSAI's surplus food guidance; vendor and NGO accept a donation declaration at onboarding.
• Three verified food-quality reports in 30 days auto-pauses a vendor pending admin review.
• Terms make clear the vendor remains the food business operator; the platform is an intermediary.
Trust and abuse controls
• Per-customer purchase cap (FR-R3) and device fingerprinting to stop bulk resale.
• Vendor reliability score (cancellations, oversell, ratings) shown to admin; low scores lose feed ranking.
• Customers with 3 no-shows in 30 days need to prepay a deposit or are paused for 7 days.
• Two-way ratings: vendors can flag abusive customers.
10. Business model and unit economics
Revenue comes from a commission per paid order, with free sign-up and no fees on charity donations. The model mirrors proven surplus marketplaces: Too Good To Go charges partners an annual fee plus a commission per bag (Too Good To Go FAQ); in the UK that is a fixed fee under £4.40 and 25% above it (Startups.co.uk). RescueBite drops the annual fee to lower the barrier for small Indian vendors.

Illustrative order: Bakery box worth ₹450, rescue price ₹180. Customer pays ₹185. Vendor receives ₹144 (₹180 − ₹36 commission) for food that would have earned ₹0. Platform earns ₹36 + ₹5 − ~₹4 gateway = ~₹37 gross margin per order.
Assumption: at 300 vendors × 8 paid orders/day × 30 days = 72,000 orders/month, gross margin is about ₹26.6 lakh/month. These figures are planning estimates to validate in the pilot.
11. Release plan
A 12-week MVP build, an 8-week invite-only pilot, then city-wide launch. Supply goes in before demand: vendors are onboarded before customers are invited.
Team for the MVP: 1 PM, 1 designer, 2 mobile engineers, 2 backend engineers, 1 QA, 1 vendor-ops lead, 2 field sales for vendor onboarding. Dates assume the build starts in December 2026.
